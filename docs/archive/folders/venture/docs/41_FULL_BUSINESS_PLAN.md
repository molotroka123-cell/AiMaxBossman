# BOSSMAN Venture Engine — Full business plan

## 1. Business thesis

The first business is not Venture Engine itself.

Venture Engine is internal capital-allocation infrastructure.

It should increase the probability that the portfolio launches fewer but better
products, reaches payment earlier and kills weak ideas sooner.

The portfolio starts Czech-first because local language, regulation,
administrative fragmentation and a large microbusiness population create
market niches where a small team can compete without needing global consumer
scale.

## 2. Customer strategy

The first portfolio should prefer:
- OSVČ;
- microbusiness 1–10 people;
- small operationally complex SMEs;
- vertical professionals with recurring administration.

The engine itself may later become sellable, but internal use is the default.

## 3. Value creation

Venture Engine creates value in five ways.

### Better selection

Do not spend months on ideas with no payer.

### Cheaper learning

Use interviews, concierge delivery, paid pilots and low-cost acquisition tests
before engineering.

### Faster revenue

Measure Time to Revenue.

### Better capital allocation

Compare ventures through contribution economics and engineering effort.

### Compounding learning

Evidence-backed commercial lessons survive the individual venture.

## 4. Competitive advantage

The product portfolio should not rely on “we use GPT”.

Potential advantage:
- Czech regulatory understanding;
- workflow depth;
- integration with local business systems;
- accumulated customer/operational data;
- fast internal development;
- local-language UX;
- reusable BOSSMAN agents;
- shared research/evidence infrastructure.

## 5. Initial portfolio strategy

Year 1:
- >=100 opportunities screened;
- >=20 deep decision packets;
- >=8 real experiments;
- >=3 concepts with paid validation;
- <=3 products launched;
- one meaningful recurring-revenue winner.

Management stretch target:
100k–250k CZK combined MRR by month 12.

This is a target, not forecast.

## 6. Product categories preferred

### Compliance/trigger software

Useful when a new obligation changes process.

### Workflow compression

Replace e-mail + Excel + PDF + calendar + manual follow-up.

### Service-to-software

Agent helps humans deliver paid output manually first.

### Monitoring/risk

Recurring alerts where missing an event costs money.

### Vertical operations

Own a complete process rather than a single feature.

## 7. Revenue strategy

Primary:
recurring subscription.

Secondary:
- onboarding;
- usage;
- setup;
- service-assisted pilot.

Avoid early dependence on:
- ads;
- speculative transaction fees;
- inventory;
- long public-sector procurement.

## 8. Pricing philosophy

Low-ticket Czech invoicing software demonstrates that a shallow admin feature
can already be cheap.

Therefore new products must capture greater workflow value.

Price should be anchored to:
- time saved;
- revenue accelerated;
- loss avoided;
- compliance labor removed;
- employee capacity released.

## 9. Gross margin

The portfolio should favor software gross margins.

Target:
- >85% low-compute mature;
- >70% AI-heavy early;
- >80% AI-heavy mature;
- >50% service-assisted pilot with credible automation path.

## 10. Customer acquisition

Priority:
high-intent channels.

Early:
- search;
- SEO;
- partner;
- referral;
- marketplace;
- inbound;
- paid validation.

Not:
mass scraped e-mail.

## 11. Sales motion

Micro-SaaS:
self-serve.

Vertical SMB:
owner-led sales, onboarding, then product-led expansion.

High-complexity:
paid concierge pilot before software.

## 12. Validation economics

A venture should earn the right to receive more capital.

Typical progression:

0–1k CZK:
research.

<=5k:
experiment.

<=20k:
limited pre-payment work.

>50k:
investment review.

This constrains downside while keeping learning fast.

## 13. First revenue objective

For low-ticket/SMB:
real external payment <=45 days after validation approval.

A product still “preparing infrastructure” after 45 days should be reviewed.

## 14. Business structure

Research personally.

Once there is recurring paid commercial activity:
operate through correct business status and proper records.

Future move to s.r.o. is triggered by:
- revenue;
- liability;
- hiring;
- enterprise demands;
- IP;
- VAT;
- investors.

Not by prestige.

## 15. Financial control

No autonomous treasury.

Agent can:
- analyse;
- reconcile;
- forecast;
- prepare.

Human approves:
- outbound money;
- contracts;
- tax actions;
- material refunds;
- campaigns.

## 16. Cash planning

Each venture:
- opening cash;
- collections;
- direct cost;
- shared allocation;
- ending cash;
- committed spend;
- tax reserve.

Portfolio:
- burn;
- runway;
- contribution.

## 17. Milestones

25k MRR:
early repeat demand.

50k:
fund core stack.

100k:
business-structure/security review.

250k:
team/portfolio discipline.

500k:
repeatable sales/support.

1m:
company-level management/cross-border option.

## 18. Team plan

Do not hire ahead of evidence.

Initial:
owner + AI stack.

Then contractors for:
- design;
- niche sales/research;
- legal/accounting;
- support.

First full-time role should remove a measured bottleneck.

## 19. Risk model

Top portfolio risks:
- building before selling;
- false market-size confidence;
- unlawful distribution;
- model/API dependency;
- product support becoming consulting;
- regulatory timing changes;
- accounting/tax confusion;
- customer concentration;
- channel concentration;
- too many concurrent ventures.

Every risk must have a metric/trigger.

## 20. 12-month operating cadence

Weekly:
- venture metrics;
- customer evidence;
- spend.

Monthly:
- allocation;
- pipeline;
- cash.

Quarterly:
- market thesis;
- legal-entity status;
- security maturity;
- venture kill/scale decisions.

## 21. Long-term outcome

The desired business is a portfolio that can repeatedly:
discover -> validate -> ship -> monetize.

Venture Engine itself becomes an intangible operating asset:
- evidence;
- process;
- templates;
- memory;
- validated skills;
- distribution knowledge;
- customer/market understanding.

The end state is not “an AI that makes money by itself”.

It is a controlled system that repeatedly finds economically valuable software
opportunities and improves its commercial decision process from real outcomes.
